## What happened

I had just finished my bachelor's. I had studied at the English language faculty, and my IELTS score was very good. I had everything I needed for a master's in Korea; all that was left was to submit the documents. Truth be told, I didn't even need a consultant: I could easily have got into any university by applying on my own. But I wanted to be sure there were no mistakes in my documents, so I used a consulting service.

---

++Service fee: $500++

To be honest, the regrets started before I had even applied. I hired the agency so that they would prepare the documents for me, yet I was the one running around: one day for a translation, the next for the motivation letter.

They were so irresponsible that I was the one reminding them the application deadline was slipping away. All I got back was "don't worry".

++Long story short++, I prepared every document they asked for, and the application was sent. The application period closed. A week later the agency got in touch: my birth certificate had been left out and still had to be submitted. "But hasn't the admission period ended?" -- "Don't worry, we'll make it in time." We didn't...

Right up until the results came out, the agency stayed perfectly calm. I was the one who was worried, and with good reason: I wasn't accepted... They didn't even review my application, because something was missing. It hurt a lot. It wasn't the money I regretted, but the six months of my life I had lost.


## Second attempt

The next semester I went to another consulting agency. This time, though, I knew better: I talked things through with them properly before agreeing to anything. Their fee was much lower, too --- 2 million soums.

This time everything was different:
- my documents went to more universities
- every document was prepared perfectly
- both the service and the attitude were excellent

The result matched the effort. I was admitted everywhere I applied, and at last my Korean adventure began.


## The takeaway

What's done is done, but the lesson stays with you for life. Not everyone who calls themselves a consultant deserves your trust. To them we are just one more client, but their mistakes can cost us our most precious resource: time.
