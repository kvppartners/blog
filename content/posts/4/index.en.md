## Introduction

"To extend my visa, they asked me for a document proving that `the money shown in my bank balance certificate came from my family`. But I had actually borrowed that money from acquaintances here in Korea and put together a ++one-day balance++. How am I supposed to prove anything now?" --- this is one of the problems that keeps coming up again and again with D-2 visa extensions.

In this article we will talk about ==prevention rather than cure==. In other words, how you can start preparing now so that you never end up in the situation above. How do you avoid leaving open questions about your bank balance or your *sponsor*? Why is a one-day balance not a solution in itself? What can you do so that nobody asks you for a bank balance certificate in the first place? By the end of this post you will have answers to all of these questions. If you are on a D-2 visa and have no legal income to cover your expenses, this advice is for you.

++As for the situation above++: if you are already in it, the right way out depends on whose name (or names) the money came from when you prepared your certificate, so it will differ from person to person. In general, though, it can be resolved with documents proving that the deposits came from a trustworthy *sponsor*, together with a letter of explanation.


## General information

### Who needs a bank balance certificate?

It is not mandatory for everyone. As a rule, students with a C grade or higher (++a CGPA above 2.0++) are exempt. But the rule is not applied the same way to everyone. Depending on the situation, an immigration officer has the right to ask anyone, regardless of their grades, for their bank balance and their transaction history for the past 6 months. To put it more plainly, the following factors may lead to you being asked for a bank balance certificate:

- Which country you are a citizen of, and how your fellow nationals are generally viewed by Korean immigration
- Which university you attend and which program you are enrolled in
- How many courses you are taking this semester
- How your classes are spread across the week

In short, anything in your documents that makes the officer wonder how you spend your time outside of your studies counts against you. Just look in the mirror, imagine you are the immigration officer, and judge yourself by the criteria above. If you come to the conclusion that you will probably need a bank balance certificate, make sure you take our advice on board.

### About the one-day balance

++I'll keep it short++: if you are thinking "I'll just put together a balance at the last minute and hand it in", you are on the wrong track! Drop that idea.

The immigration officer does not care that you had 10 or 20 million won in your account yesterday or a week ago. What they care about is where that money came from, and whether you will really be living on it for the next year. But the officer cannot check your account every month for the next year. Since they cannot check the future, the only thing left is to draw conclusions from your bank history. If, over the past 6 months or a year, you really have been receiving money from your family and living on it, you will most likely keep doing the same over the next year, and that is the real proof. So a one-day balance will not help you; what matters is what your bank history has looked like over the past year. Which means preparing for your bank balance certificate starts not a week before, but a year before!

As mentioned above, a bank balance certificate is not mandatory. If your grades are above the minimum requirement, you can simply go without one. But it is important to always be ready for the "++what if they ask?++" scenario. If you keep your bank records "*neat*", the certificate will never be something you lose sleep over. If they ask for it, you simply get it once they ask. If they want your bank history as well, you print that out too. If they ask where the money came from, you prepare that as well. In short, once you are ready for the worst case, the words *what if* will no longer worry you.

## A *neat* bank account

For a student, a neat bank account means one large deposit a month and small everyday expenses. The deposit does not even have to be monthly: it could be weekly, or once every 6 months. What matters is where it comes from. Everyone's situation is different, but everyone spends money to live, and that money comes from somewhere. If you:

- have personal income that you can legally prove
- have family members in Korea who earn a legal income
- have your expenses paid by a third party, a *sponsor*
- receive some kind of scholarship

or can prove in any other lawful way that your expenses are covered, then this is not a problem for you. You simply show who or where that "income" comes from, and that's it -- no more questions.

++What if there is no legal income?++ Then you rely on income that makes sense. One of the easiest ways is this: every time you get cash in hand, deposit it onto your bank card at an ATM and pay your monthly expenses from that money. When you are asked for your bank history, it shows cash deposits and small expenses. Of course, if your account shows nothing but cash deposits for a whole year, that may raise some suspicion. But it is still far easier than having to prove who a stranger who sent you money is to you. Cash can come from many different sources, and proving where it came from is entirely in your hands. ++But don't try to lie!++ You are sitting across from an officer with years of experience who reads people like a psychologist. Your goal is to prepare documents that leave the officer with no questions and raise no suspicion. If questions do come up, answer as truthfully as you can, and if extra documents are requested, back up your answers with solid evidence.

++From experience++. I have seen many cases where a D-2 visa was extended without any bank balance certificate at all, just by showing 6 months of bank history, and I have tried it myself. If you can show that for the past 6 months you have been receiving money month after month from a credible "source", the officer is not interested in how much money you have today. You may not have enough in your account right now, but there is solid evidence that you have confirmed income coming in next month. That counts for far more than a one-day balance put together just for show.
